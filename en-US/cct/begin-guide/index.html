The Guide For Beginner

CCT and MEF#

The Complete Coordinate Calculation Tutorial for Minecraft Bedrock Edition

About This Book#

In the pure-command environment of Minecraft Bedrock Edition (MCBE), there are two fundamentally distinct techniques. The community often conflates them, making both harder to grasp.

CCT (Coordinates Calculation Theory / Coordinate Computation Technique, historically known in the community as the "Large-Number Compass") solves the problem of computation. It uses the virtual positions and rotations constructed via /execute to turn the game engine into a calculator—computing midpoints, mirrored points, rotated points, and even squaring, multiplication, and division. When a CCT command finishes running, it outputs one single, precise point.

MEF (Multi-Execute Fork, commonly referred to as the "n-ary tree") solves the problem of duplication. It leverages the property that when a target selector matches multiple entities, execution forks along multiple paths, allowing a single command to execute simultaneously across hundreds or thousands of positions. MEF performs no arithmetic on its own; it simply multiplies execution paths.

In a single sentence:

CCT is responsible for computing a point; MEF is responsible for turning a single computation into many points.

This book cleanly decouples the two. Chapters 1 through 5 cover CCT, where all selector arguments strictly include c=1 (single execution path, one point per command). Chapter 6 is dedicated to MEF. Chapter 7 combines them—shapes like circles and ellipses are the product of this combination. Chapter 8 translates the geometric intuition into rigorous algebraic expressions, followed by a formula reference in the Appendix.

Everything before Chapter 6 requires only junior-high math: similar triangles, the Pythagorean theorem, the difference-of-squares formula, and the inscribed angle theorem. Chapter 8 introduces vectors and matrices.

Chapter 1　The Coordinate Syntax of execute#

At bottom, CCT does only three things: change the origin, change the axes, and move along the axes. This chapter makes the syntax for those three things clear.

1.1 Three coordinate systems#

There are three ways to write coordinates in Minecraft, differing in what they are measured against.

Absolute Coordinates#

Directly specifies world coordinates:

Regardless of where or which way you look, the destination is identical.

Relative Coordinates ~#

Offsets relative to the current execution origin:

X plus 5, Y plus 2, Z minus 3. A bare ~ equals ~0:

Spawns a particle 1 block above the current origin.

Local Coordinates ^#

Offsets relative to the current view rotation. The three parameters are always ordered:

Move 5 blocks forward along the current line of sight.

Spawns a particle 1 block to the left and 2 blocks forward relative to current view heading.

Mixing Rules#

Within a single 3-axis parameter group, ~ and absolute coordinates can be mixed:

^ cannot be mixed with either. positioned ^1 ~2 ^3 is invalid syntax.

To combine them, split into two subcommands:

First raise the world coordinate by 1, then advance along the local line of sight by 3. This chaining pattern appears everywhere in CCT.

1.2 Rotation: yaw and pitch#

Rotation is expressed as two numbers:

yaw is horizontal rotation:

pitch is vertical elevation:

Because the direction of ^ is determined entirely by rotation, ^ ^ ^1 is affected by pitch and is not necessarily horizontally forward. Looking up by 30° makes ^ ^ ^1 move forward and upward simultaneously.

Three immediately useful observations:

  1. The local left direction is always horizontal. The left vector depends solely on yaw; pitch has no effect on it. Thus positioned ^5 ^ ^ is always a pure horizontal translation.
  2. Adding 180° to yaw flips only horizontal components. The X and Z components of the forward vector are negated, while Y remains invariant. The squaring formula in Chapter 4 and the ellipse mapping in Chapter 7 rely on this property.
  3. Entity pitch cannot reach ±90°. Bedrock entities have a hard clamp on pitch, preventing them from looking truly straight up or down. A clean displacement along the Y axis cannot be achieved by pointing an entity vertically; alternative techniques are required.

1.3 Five core subcommands#

CCT relies primarily on five subcommands with clean divisions of labor.

positioned: changes position only#

Note that positioned as moves the position only; it does not copy rotation.

facing: changes rotation only#

Points the line of sight toward a target point or entity. feet targets the feet and eyes targets the eyes. Do not mix these within the same formula, or an offset of ~1.62 blocks will distort your calculations.

rotated: changes rotation directly via angles#

Four notations must be strictly distinguished:

rotated ~ 22.5UnchangedSet absolutely to 22.5
rotated ~22.5 ~Add 22.5 to currentUnchanged

rotated ~ 0UnchangedSet absolutely to 0
rotated 22.5 0Set absolutely to 22.5Set absolutely to 0

Rule of thumb: Numbers with ~ are relative; numbers without ~ are absolute. A bare ~ keeps that angle component unchanged.

at: changes both position and rotation#

Transfers both origin and orientation to the target entity. CCT commands frequently use this as the starting anchor.

align: block-grid flooring#

Floors coordinates to integers along specified axes. Combined with large-number translation, it implements modulo operations and periodic wrapping (used in Appendix D.3 for trigonometric functions).

1.4 The Single-Target Principle#

Every pure CCT command must produce exactly one output point. To guarantee this, all selectors must be written with strict constraints:

  • type=armor_stand: Restricts entity type to prevent unintended matching.
  • name=pointA: Identifies the specific entity role.
  • c=1: Caps target selection to at most 1 entity.

If a selector matches 3 entities, the downstream chain executes 3 times. While multi-branching forms the basis of MEF (Chapter 6), it causes confusion in CCT. Therefore, Chapters 1 through 5 strictly enforce c=1.

Chapter 2　How CCT Works#

This chapter explains why traversing along a view rotation paired with a large distance computes precise geometric ratio points.

2.1 What facing actually does#

Let the current position be P and the target be Q. When facing is executed, orientation points toward Q. Traversing d blocks:

places execution at a point starting from P toward Q of length exactly d.

Regardless of whether P and Q are 3 blocks or 3000 blocks apart, facing normalizes the displacement vector (Q - P) into a unit vector of length 1.

facing gives us a normalized direction that encapsulates the relative geometric ratio between P and Q. CCT extracts this ratio by constructing right triangles and applying similar triangles.

2.2 Finding midpoints with similar triangles#

Let S and M be two points at identical height separated by horizontal distance D. We want their midpoint.

  1. Step 1: Elevate the origin above S by a large distance H to S′.
  2. Step 2: From S′, look at M. The line of sight forms the hypotenuse:
    L = \sqrt{H^2 + D^2}
  3. Step 3: Advance H/2 along the hypotenuse.
    The small triangle formed is similar to \triangle S'SM with similarity ratio \frac{H/2}{L}.
    Horizontal movement:
    \Delta x = \frac{H/2}{L}\times D = \frac{HD}{2L}

    Vertical descent:
    \Delta y_{\text{drop}} = \frac{H/2}{L}\times H = \frac{H^2}{2L}
  4. Step 4: When H \gg D:
    L = \sqrt{H^2+D^2} = H\sqrt{1+\frac{D^2}{H^2}} \approx H

    Substituting into the horizontal displacement:
    \Delta x \approx \frac{HD}{2H} = \frac{D}{2}

    This is exactly half the horizontal distance. The vertical drop is \frac{H^2}{2H} = H/2, so descending by relative coordinate H/2 returns exactly to the starting height.

2.3 Generalizing to arbitrary ratios#

Replacing H/2 with tH:

\text{Horizontal forward: } \Delta x \approx tD

\text{Vertical drop: } \Delta y_{\text{drop}} \approx tH

Dropping from elevation H by tH leaves height at (1-t)H.

The Fundamental CCT Ratio Formula:

\text{Forward: } tH, \qquad \text{Compensate Drop: } (1-t)H

  • t = 0.5: Midpoint
  • t = 0.25: Quarter-point
  • t = 1.0: Target point M itself
  • t > 1.0 or t < 0: Points on the line extension

2.4 The compass analogy and the role of the large number#

In compass-and-straightedge constructions, geometric intersections are found using circles of fixed radius. In CCT, we use a fixed large distance H as a radial baseline. Because the approximation L \approx H requires H \gg D, H must be far larger than the figure's scale.

2.5 Geometric truncation error#

Expanding \sqrt{H^2+D^2} via binomial approximation (1+u)^{-1/2} \approx 1 - u/2:

\frac{tHD}{\sqrt{H^2+D^2}} \approx tD\left(1 - \frac{D^2}{2H^2}\right) = tD - \frac{tD^3}{2H^2}

Horizontal Truncation Error:

E_x \approx \frac{tD^3}{2H^2}

Vertical Truncation Error:

E_y \approx \frac{tD^2}{2H}

Key Properties:

  1. Horizontal error grows as the cube of distance D (D^3).
  2. Vertical error decays as 1/H, while horizontal error decays as 1/H^2. Vertical error decays slower and becomes visible first.

For t = 0.5, H = 100000:

DHorizontal Error E_xVertical Error E_y
100.0000000250.00025
1000.0000250.025
10000.0252.5

2.6 Floating-point error and choosing H#

Floating-point representations have fixed relative precision \varepsilon \approx 2^{-23}. Total error balances geometric truncation against floating-point roundoff:

E(H) \approx C_1\frac{r^2}{H} + C_2\varepsilon H

Error is minimized when both terms are equal:
H \sim \frac{r}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}} \approx 2900\,r

For structures spanning a few to dozens of blocks, H \in [1024, 16384] is optimal.

Synchronous Replacement Principle: When changing H, all corresponding distances (H, tH, (1-t)H, 2H) across the formula must be updated simultaneously.

2.7 Summary#

CCT relies on the asymptotic approximation \sqrt{H^2+D^2} \approx H for H \gg D. It is an asymptotic numerical method, and H must be chosen within an optimal range.

Chapter 4　Advanced CCT#

Chaining multiple facing operations enables higher-order geometric operations: reflections, rotations, and arithmetic.

4.1 Far-point reflection#

Far-point reflection is the core primitive of advanced CCT. It mirrors a point across an arbitrary axis line passing through the origin.

Canonical Skeleton:

Command (H = 1024):

Mathematical Form:
Let origin be (0,0,0), point vector be \mathbf{p}, and axis unit vector be \mathbf{u}:

\mathbf{p}_H = \mathbf{p} + 2H\mathbf{u} - 2H\frac{\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}}{\lVert\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}\rVert}

As H \to \infty:
\mathbf{p}' = 2(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{p})\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{p}

The component parallel to \mathbf{u} is preserved, while the component perpendicular to \mathbf{u} is inverted.

Invariant Distance Property:

\lVert\mathbf{p}'\rVert = \lVert\mathbf{p}\rVert

Reflection preserves distance from the origin.

4.2 Two reflections equal one rotation#

Composing reflections across two axes intersecting at angle \theta yields a rotation around their intersection by 2\theta, independent of the point's initial coordinates.

4.3 Squaring#

Formula: (x,y,z) \to (x, y+x^2, z)

Derivation:
Starting at (x, y, z), facing 0 ~a ~ sets direction \mathbf{F} = \frac{(-x, a, 0)}{s} where s = \sqrt{a^2+x^2}.

  1. positioned ^ ^ ^-a² applies displacement \Delta_1 = \left(\frac{a^2x}{s}, -\frac{a^3}{s}, 0\right).
  2. rotated ~180 ~ flips horizontal components: \mathbf{F}' = \left(\frac{x}{s}, \frac{a}{s}, 0\right).
  3. positioned ^ ^ ^-a² applies \Delta_2 = \left(-\frac{a^2x}{s}, -\frac{a^3}{s}, 0\right).

Sum of displacements:

\Delta x = 0, \qquad \Delta y = -\frac{2a^3}{s} = -2a^2\left(1+\frac{x^2}{a^2}\right)^{-1/2} \approx -2a^2 + x^2

  1. positioned ~ ~2a² ~ cancels the constant -2a^2, leaving \Delta y = x^2.

4.4 Multiplication#

Using the difference-of-squares identity:

xz = \frac{(x+z)^2 - (x-z)^2}{4}

Alternatively, using 2D vector hypotenuse normalization (6 subcommands):

Function: (x,y,z) \to (?, y+xz, ?).

4.5 Division#

Normalization in facing computes division by the hypotenuse:

\frac{(x,y,z)}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}}

Short division formula: (x,y,z) \to (?, ?, x/z)

Here a is large and b is small. As z \to 0, error diverges asymptotically; inputs must be bounded away from zero.

4.6 Higher powers#

  • x^3 = x^2 \cdot x
  • x^4 = x^2 \cdot x^2 (superior precision compared to x^3 \cdot x)

4.7 Coordinate scaling#

Before applying arithmetic formulas, scale inputs to a compact domain like [-1, 1]:

\text{Scale Input} \to \text{Compute Operation} \to \text{Rescale Result}

4.8 Error engineering#

  • Geometric Truncation Error: E_g \approx C_1 \frac{r^2}{H}
  • Floating-Point Error: E_f \approx C_2 \varepsilon H

Sweep H \in \{256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192\} to find empirical minimums.

Chapter 5　CCT Applications#

5.1 The boundary of CCT#

A pure CCT command runs with c=1 and outputs one single spatial point. It acts as a geometric and arithmetic calculator. Generating multi-point point clouds requires MEF.

5.2 Plotting parabolas#

To draw y = x^2:

  • Multi-Block Method: Multiple command blocks evaluate discrete inputs x \in [-4, 4] in parallel.
  • Moving Input Method: An armor stand moves continuously along the X axis, evaluating y = x^2 each tick.

5.3 Dot product: projection length#

The dot product \mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{u} represents the scalar projection of \mathbf{p} along unit vector \mathbf{u}.

The midpoint between point P and its reflection across axis dir is the orthogonal projection of P onto dir.

5.4 Cross product: normal vectors#

Computes \mathbf{p} \times \mathbf{A} yielding magnitude and orthogonal direction (Appendix C.2).

5.5 Coordinate frame alignment#

Aligns the global vertical ~ ~1 ~ axis with entity @s's view direction ^ ^ ^1 (Appendix C.5).

5.6 Summary#

CCT serves as an on-demand spatial calculator. To scale a single computation into thousands of points, we introduce MEF.

Chapter 6　MEF: The n-ary Tree#

6.1 Origins of the name#

MEF (Multi-Execute Fork) is also known as the n-ary tree or conjugate bisection. It exploits execution branching across multiple selected entities.

6.2 The forking mechanism#

When a selector in as, at, positioned as, rotated as, or facing entity matches T entities, the entire downstream execution chain runs T times independently.

Chaining k selection stages where each stage matches n_j entities produces:

N = \prod_{j=1}^k n_j

For k binary stages (n=2), N = 2^k leaf executions. In MEF, selectors must not include c=1.

6.4 The binary weight formula#

To distribute 2^k points uniformly across [-L, L], layer weights must follow a binary descending sequence:

w_j = L\cdot\frac{2^{k-1-j}}{2^k-1}, \qquad j = 0, 1, \ldots, k-1

Point spacing:
\Delta = \frac{2L}{2^k-1}

Chapter 7　CCT × MEF#

7.1 Division of labor#

MEF multiplies execution paths; CCT performs geometric transformations along each path.

7.2 The nested point lattice circle#

Based on the Inscribed Angle Theorem.

  • Center entity: a
  • Diameter endpoints: two entities named r at distance R from a.

Repeating the nested unit doubles point density while preserving angular uniformity.

7.3 The angle bisection circle#

Uses MEF for binary branching and CCT far-point reflections with halving angle sequences (45^\circ, 22.5^\circ, 11.25^\circ, 5.625^\circ, 2.8125^\circ, 1.40625^\circ):

Because far-point reflection preserves origin distance \lVert\mathbf{p}\rVert = 2, points remain on a sphere of radius 2. Confined to X=0, they form a circle. Changing the starting position from 0 2 0 to 0 R 0 modifies the radius.

7.4 From circle to ellipse#

Appending a non-uniform mapping onto the circle:

  1. facing 0 0 0 + positioned 0 0 0: Converts position into line-of-sight orientation while resetting coordinates to (0,0,0).
  2. positioned ^ ^ ^4: Advances 4 along orientation.
  3. rotated ~180 ~: Inverts horizontal yaw components while leaving vertical pitch invariant.
  4. positioned ^ ^ ^-2: Advances -2.
\text{Y Semi-Axis} = |d_1 + d_2| = |4 + (-2)| = 2
\text{Z Semi-Axis} = |d_1 - d_2| = |4 - (-2)| = 6

7.5 Structural anatomy of other figures#

Standard composition pipeline:

  1. MEF Forking: Expand execution branches.
  2. Seed Constraints: Establish initial coordinate offsets.
  3. CCT Reflections: Replicate and rotate points.
  4. Non-Uniform Mapping (Optional): Reshape profiles.

7.6 Summary#

Use MEF for affine grid structures (lines, planes, boxes). Use CCT for rotations, reflections, and non-linear mappings.

Chapter 8　Extension: Algebraic Expression of CCT#

8.1 State and rotation matrices#

The state of an execute chain is:

\text{State} = (\mathbf{P}, \mathbf{R})

where \mathbf{P} = (x, y, z)^T and \mathbf{R} = [\mathbf{L} \quad \mathbf{U} \quad \mathbf{F}].

\mathbf{R} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos ry & -\sin rx\sin ry & -\cos rx\sin ry \\ 0 & \cos rx & -\sin rx \\ \sin ry & \sin rx\cos ry & \cos rx\cos ry \end{bmatrix}
\mathbf{L} = (\cos ry, 0, \sin ry)^T
\mathbf{U} = (-\sin rx\sin ry, \cos rx, \sin rx\cos ry)^T
\mathbf{F} = (-\cos rx\sin ry, -\sin rx, \cos rx\cos ry)^T

Conversion to standard mathematical coordinates:

(x,y,z)_{\text{MC}} \to (z,x,y)_{\text{Math}}, \qquad (ry, rx)_{\text{MC}} \to (-ry, -rx)_{\text{Math}}

8.2 Algebraic forms of subcommands#

  • positioned ^a ^b ^c:
    \mathbf{P} \to \mathbf{P} + a\mathbf{L} + b\mathbf{U} + c\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{P} + \mathbf{R}\begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{bmatrix}
  • facing <target>:
    Sets \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{P}_{\text{target}} - \mathbf{P}, then:
    \mathbf{F} \to \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert}, \qquad \mathbf{U} \to \frac{\mathbf{Y}-(\mathbf{Y}\cdot\mathbf{F})\mathbf{F}}{\lVert\mathbf{Y}-(\mathbf{Y}\cdot\mathbf{F})\mathbf{F}\rVert}, \qquad \mathbf{L} \to \mathbf{U}\times\mathbf{F}
  • rotated ~a ~b:
    \mathbf{R} \to \mathbf{R} \cdot M(a, b)

8.3 Rigorous derivation of far-point reflection#

For origin at (0,0,0), point \mathbf{p}, axis unit vector \mathbf{u}, and parameter H:

  1. rotated as axis: \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{u}
  2. positioned ^ ^ ^H: \mathbf{P}_1 = \mathbf{p} + H\mathbf{u}
  3. facing 0 0 0: \mathbf{F}_1 = -\frac{\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}}{\lVert\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}\rVert}
  4. positioned ^ ^ ^2H: \mathbf{P}_2 = \mathbf{p} + H\mathbf{u} - 2H\frac{\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}}{\lVert\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}\rVert}
  5. rotated as axis: \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{u}
  6. positioned ^ ^ ^H:
    \mathbf{p}_H = \mathbf{p} + 2H\mathbf{u} - 2H\frac{\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}}{\lVert\mathbf{p}+H\mathbf{u}\rVert}

Decomposing \mathbf{p} = (\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{p})\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{w} where \mathbf{w} \perp \mathbf{u}:

\lim_{H\to\infty} \mathbf{p}_H = 2(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{p})\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{p}

Matrix representation:
S_\mathbf{u} = 2\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}^T - I

8.4 What transformation is a reflection, really?#

\det(S_\mathbf{u}) = (+1)(-1)(-1) = +1

S_\mathbf{u} is an orthogonal transformation with determinant +1; in 3D Euclidean space, it represents a 180^\circ rotation about axis \mathbf{u}.

Composition of two reflections:

R = S_\mathbf{v}S_\mathbf{u} \implies \text{Rotation by } 2\theta \text{ around axis } \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}

8.5 Converting back to in-game angles#

ry = \operatorname{atan2}(\sin ry, \cos ry) \times \frac{180}{\pi}
rx = \operatorname{atan2}(\sin rx, \cos rx) \times \frac{180}{\pi}

8.6 Limits of the algebraic approach#

Algebraic models describe theoretical limits as H \to \infty. Practical deployment requires numeric validation to account for finite floating-point precision.

8.7 Summary#

  • Nonlinear operations in CCT originate from the denominator \lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert during vector normalization in facing.
  • Reflection operator S_\mathbf{u} = 2\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}^T - I rotates by 180^\circ around \mathbf{u}.

Epilogue#

This text unifies two core disciplines:

CCT is built upon four fundamental actions:

  • positioned: Shift the virtual evaluation frame.
  • rotated: Transform the local coordinate basis.
  • facing: Normalize displacements into orientation vectors.
  • Large Number H: Approximate nonlinear radical normalizations into linear and quadratic relations.

MEF is encapsulated in a single principle:

A selector matching T entities forks downstream execution into T independent concurrent paths.

Combining both allows the construction of sophisticated 3D curves, surfaces, and functional geometric computing systems in vanilla Minecraft Bedrock Edition.
